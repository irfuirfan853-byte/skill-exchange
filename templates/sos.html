🔐 Staying safe — and how we protect your data

Most emergencies on a learning platform start with a message, a file or a shared contact detail. These habits keep you safe while you teach and learn.

🔑 Your password and codes

  • Use a password you don't use anywhere else.
  • Never share your password — not even with someone who says they are an admin.
  • Reset codes (OTP) are for you only. Nobody legitimate will ever ask you for one.
  • On a shared or public computer, always log out when you finish.

👤 Verify before you trust

  • Check the profile, skills and certificates before agreeing to an exchange.
  • Use the voice or video call to confirm who you are talking to.
  • Be careful with people who rush you, ask for money, or move the chat off the platform immediately.

📎 Files, links and personal details

  • Don't open unexpected files or links — even from someone you are learning with.
  • Share only what the exchange needs: no ID cards, bank details or home address.
  • Never send money to a “teacher” or “learner”, and never pay to unlock a certificate.

🚨 In an emergency

  • Contact your local emergency services first — this page is not a replacement.
  • Send an SOS here so the admin team can act on the account side (block, review, preserve evidence).
  • Mark the report resolved once you are safe, so the team knows you no longer need help.

What an SOS stores, and who can see it

Saved with your reportEmergency type, your description, optional location and contact, timestamps, status
Who can read itOnly you and platform admins — never other members, never public pages, not indexed by search engines
What admins can doAcknowledge, add a note you can see, resolve, reopen, or call you back
Call privacyAudio/video flows directly between you and the admin (peer-to-peer); the connection details used to set the call up are deleted when it ends
Your passwordStored only as a hash — nobody, including admins, can read it. Reset codes are hashed too and expire in 10 minutes

Admins never message you asking for a password, an OTP, or a payment. If that happens, send an SOS with the type Account or data security and stop replying.
